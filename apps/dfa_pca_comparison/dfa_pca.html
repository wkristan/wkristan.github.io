DFA and PCA

There are some clear similarities between DFA and PCA, so much so that sometimes researchers will conduct PCA and then use PCA scores in an ANOVA to compare groups. If you want to compare groups, though, a DFA is a better choice - click on the LD1, PC1 and PC2 entries in the legend for the scatter plot on the left to see why this is the case.

With two groups the LD1 goes through the group centroids, and it is all that is needed to separate the groups as well as possible. Scores along LD1 for the two groups are non-overlapping.

The first PCA axis is positioned to maximally account for variation in the data, not separation between groups. The fairly strong correlation between minor axis and height is what determines where PC1 falls, and it does not separate the groups well.

The second PCA axis is positioned to account for variation in the data independent of the first. There is no overlap between the groups along PC2 either, but since it also is not placed to go through the centroids there is no guarantee that any component will separate the groups well.
